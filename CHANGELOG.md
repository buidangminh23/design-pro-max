## [0.2.1] - 2026-11-25

### Added

- A seventh own part, `skills/apple/whats-new/`: the in-app What's New popup an app shows after an update, for SwiftUI apps on macOS and iOS and for web UIs, including Tauri and Capacitor shells around React. `PART.md` first decides whether a popup is warranted at all (nothing for a fix, a tip in place for one small change, one card, a pager for two to five changes, onboarding rather than this on a first install), then the hard rules, recipes, build steps, a never-ship list, verify, report and routing; four references (`design.md`, `behaviour-and-storage.md`, `swiftui.md`, `testing.md`) and a generated `references/evidence.md`. One feature gets one card with no arrows, dots or counter; several get named previous and next buttons that are disabled at the ends rather than hidden, page dots with 24 by 24 CSS px hit areas, an "N of M" label, and a primary button that reads Next until the last page. Every page sits in one grid cell, so the card keeps one size; videos are muted, inline, have a poster and play only on the current page, a loop over five seconds gets a pause control, and under reduced motion nothing starts by itself and pages change without a transform. It shows once per version, only after an update: a first install stores the version silently, and the version is stored when the popup is dismissed, not when it opens.
- whats-new assets: `assets/lib/whats-new.js` and `whats-new.css`, a dependency-free module that builds a `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`, moves focus to the primary button, wraps Tab, restores focus, pages with the arrow keys (right to left aware), waits for the first screen and for any other rendered modal (a hidden one does not hold it back), answers a second call made while it waits or is open with the same result instead of a second popup, and takes localized labels; on a card under 520 px the auto-advance control sits in the top corner opposite the close button; the CSS-drawn references `assets/reference/whats-new-single.html` and `whats-new-pager.html` (with Vietnamese labels); and the Swift references `assets/swift/WhatsNew.swift` (the model, a `UserDefaults` version store on `CFBundleShortVersionString`, the sheet with custom dots as one adjustable VoiceOver element, the presenter that marks the version seen on dismissal and, with several windows on one store, shows the sheet in the first one only, and a "What's New…" command) and `WhatsNewVideo.swift` (a muted `AVQueuePlayer` and `AVPlayerLooper` that pauses off-page and waits for a tap under Reduce Motion), type-checked with Swift 6 for macOS 13 and 14 and the iOS 16 and 17 simulators.
- `whats-new/scripts/check-whats-new.mjs` for the 7 error rules WN-DIALOG-01, WN-NAV-01, WN-LAYOUT-01, WN-MEDIA-01, WN-MOTION-01, WN-TARGET-01 and WN-ONCE-01 and 5 warnings (page count, auto-advance, Apple's recommended target size, contrast, description length), in Playwright's Chromium or WebKit, with selector overrides for a popup that does not use the lib; it finds the page indicator in the slide label, the current dot or a counter, returns focus to the element that had it before the popup opened, watches up to 8 s for auto-advance and stops it before walking the pages, requires hidden clips to be rewound, looks for movement in the page track and page contents as well as the pages, and watches storage writes for 3 s while the popup is open; with them `rules.json` holds 39 error rules.
- `evidence/ledger.json`: 18 new own-words claims behind the whats-new rules, WH-0 to WH-17 (16 documented, 2 measured on the shipped reference and the checker runs); the 5 s pause rule reuses PV-21.
- Red-first fixtures `fixtures/whats-new/` (30 cases: 8 pass, 16 fail, 6 warn) on planted-defect pages over the shipped library; `scripts/verify.mjs` runs them, and `scripts/lib/fixture-media.mjs` builds their two silent H.264 clips (6 s and 3 s) with frame-0 posters at run time, so no media is committed. Every new error rule was seen failing on its fail fixture and passing on its pass fixtures; `test/whats-new.test.mjs` covers the checker's rule lists, arguments, helpers and a run from a copy outside the repository.
- Part evals `evals/apple/whats-new/evals.json` (cases 37 to 42: a SwiftUI single card, a SwiftUI pager with video, a Tauri and React pager, a review of a broken popup, once-per-version and a reopen item, Vietnamese labels) with their files, routing cases r58 to r60 and the near miss n26 (an Android-only What's New dialog); `evals/harness.mjs` runs the part as a suite.

### Changed

- The router routes in-app What's New popups to `whats-new`, with a routing note that keeps App Store What's New text on the asc-whats-new-writer row and teaching one control in place with TipKit in `hig`; the not-covered list names Android-only apps beside Android-only shells. To stay within 15,000 characters, several standing rules and notes are worded more tightly with no rule or route dropped, and the duplicate "never stretch a part" line is gone (standing rule 3 says it). The parts index lists 13 active parts: the 6 vendored ones and the own parts launch-scroll, launch-page, launch-video, hig, menubar-widgets, web-shells and whats-new.
- The router description puts first the SwiftUI and Swift questions Claude Code answered from its own knowledge in the v0.2 round (lists, alerts, sheets, data races, XCTest to Swift Testing, slow type-checking) and adds "even if known"; to stay within 500 characters it drops Apple Ads and Home Screen. Measured in the 0.2.1 round (`evals/RESULTS.md`): Claude Code started apple on six of those eight rows in a 15-prompt pilot; Codex kept recall at 55/56 but started on 3 of 21 near misses and made 3 partial reads, two bars it met in 0.2.0, recorded as known limits.
- The verify workflow also runs on changes under `skills/apple/whats-new/`, and `web-card.json` carries the recomputed stats (45 guides, 13 active, 39 error rules, 99 fail fixtures, 118 eval cases, 362 ledger claims).

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-24

### Added

- A third own part, `skills/apple/launch-page/`: launch and landing pages (hero, feature tiles, galleries and marquees, pricing, FAQ) built on the part's own token sets and the user's brand, or reviewed read-only, with page text treated as data. `PART.md` starts with a page read that may conclude a page needs no motion, then the modes, hard rules, recipes, build steps, a never-ship list, verify, report and routing; three references (`type-and-color.md`, `layout-and-components.md` with the English re-count of the headline band, `motion-and-intro.md`) and a generated `references/evidence.md`.
- launch-page token sets in `assets/tokens.json`, which `scripts/gen.mjs` writes into `assets/tokens.css` and the Tailwind v4 `assets/theme.tailwind.css`: restrained on `:root`, expressive with fluid hero, heading and lede sizes on `:root[data-launch-mode="expressive"]`, one reduced-motion block for the media query and `html.reduced-motion`, and `:lang()` overrides for Japanese, Korean, Thai and Chinese. Every colour pair reaches 4.5:1 in light and dark; `gen.mjs --check` catches drift.
- launch-page libraries `lib/gate.js` (motion gate, intro decision with a 3500 ms timeout, low-memory gate), `lib/reveal.js` (runs once, then stops observing) and `lib/gallery.js` (a gallery whose dot timer equals the clip length and a marquee, both with labelled pause toggles and still under reduced motion), and the CSS-drawn references `reference/launch-page.html` and `reference/intro-handoff.html`.
- `launch-page/scripts/check-page.mjs` for the 7 error rules LP-FONT-01, LP-BASE-01, LP-TYPE-01, LP-CONTRAST-01, LP-AMB-01, LP-INTRO-01 and LP-REVEAL-01 and 9 warnings: font requests and stacks, visibility without script and under reduced motion, sizes against the chosen token set and whole-pixel leading, contrast on the first opaque background and against the worst pixel of an image or gradient under the text (video backgrounds stay "cannot prove"), pause controls, the intro end state with the film blocked or `play()` refused, and reveals kept after scrolling back. Each contrast failure names a passing colour. `scripts/lint-css.mjs` is a static pass with no browser.
- Repository-only `scripts/lint-tokens.mjs`: fails a token set that matches 3 or more measured reference values within 1 px or 0.001 em, each mode and `:lang()` set on its own; the values sit hashed in `evidence/token-hashes.json` so the file is not a copyable token sheet; they are public measurements and the hashes can be reversed. It runs in `npm test` and fails a fixture that copies three values.
- A fourth own part, `skills/apple/launch-video/`: demo clips rendered from code. A Slot Read decides between a still, a live replica and a rendered clip; screen recording is not covered, and the part never asserts how a reference site made its clips. Five references (`paths-and-licenses.md`, `direction.md`, `render-html.md`, `encode-recipes.md`, `embed-component.md`) and a generated `references/evidence.md`.
- `launch-video/scripts/render-frames.mjs`: pauses the page clock and every animation, steps each frame at device pixel ratio 2, stops with the element and frame named when an image never paints, and writes frames to `$TMPDIR`; `--check` verifies a frames folder or master video and flags the browser's built-in page recording.
- `launch-video/scripts/encode.mjs`: H.264 High (always), VP9 and AV1 profiles with no audio, limited range and colour tags written with `setparams`, the moov box first, a frame-0 start image and a manifest with its version; `--alpha` writes HEVC with alpha and VP9 with alpha plus H.264 flattened over `--background`, and exits 3 with the outside-sandbox command when VideoToolbox is blocked.
- `launch-video/scripts/check-embed.mjs` and `assets/lib/demo-embed.js`: lazy clips with `preload="none"`, no autoplay attribute, a 600 px load margin, playback while half visible, a labelled pause control on every loop and no playback under reduced motion until the reader asks. By default the component also plays one clip at a time and holds every clip under Save-Data; both are choices of the component, not measured rules, and check-embed does not test them. `assets/reference/ui-mock.html` (one original mock, live and renderable on one CSS clock), `demo-embed.html` and `device-frames.css` (generic CSS frames).
- `shared/check-media.mjs --profile demo` with `--poster`, `--slot` and `--loop`, for launch-video's encode rules; the scrub profile is unchanged.
- launch-scroll image sequences: `assets/lib/frame-window.js`, a canvas player that keeps the encoded frames but decodes only a window of (2 x radius + 1) frames around the current one and closes every bitmap that leaves it, with the same gates as the film; `assets/reference/scrub-sequence.html`; `scripts/make-sequence.mjs`, which cuts a master into jpg, png, webp or avif frames with `sequence.json`, the start image and the still, prints decoded memory, the download total and the size of an equivalent GOP-6 film, and refuses a sequence over its decoded budget unless `--force`; and the advisory `scripts/bench-scrub.mjs`, which counts the frames a film and a sequence of the same clip show in headless Chromium and never fails a build.
- `rules.json`: 7 error rules and 9 warnings for launch-page, 7 error rules and 8 warnings for launch-video (direction, slot budget, density, frame rate, AV1 order, loop seam, the alpha pair and playback), so the three launch parts have 22 error rules; with the 5 of menubar-widgets and the 5 of web-shells, `rules.json` holds 32. `evidence/ledger.json`: 50 new own-words claims (36 measured, 6 documented, 6 inferred, 2 policy) behind them.
- Red-first fixtures: `fixtures/launch-page/` (30 cases, including the planted-defect page `pages/broken-launch.html` with its committed findings in `broken-launch.expected.json`) and `fixtures/launch-video/` (23 cases, among them the five bad encode recipes `yuvj420p`, 4:4:4, AAC in a muted clip, untagged and moov at the end), plus the launch-scroll `scrub-sequence` pass case and `gate-01-sequence-ignores-motion` fail case. Every new error rule was seen failing on its fail fixture and passing on its pass fixture.
- Part evals `evals/apple/launch-page/evals.json` (cases 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 17) and `evals/apple/launch-video/evals.json` (cases 11 to 15 and 20) with their fixture files, and routing cases for the launch-page, launch-video and image-sequence rows; `evals/harness.mjs` runs both parts as suites. `evals/RESULTS.md` records the v0.2 round: every own part beats its no-skill baseline on both hosts, and the routing bars are scored at high effort as in v0.1. Codex meets every routing bar; Claude Code meets all but recall (45/53, 84.9 %), which is recorded as a known limit in `evals/RESULTS.md` and the README.

- A fifth own part, `skills/apple/menubar-widgets/`: macOS menu bar apps and WidgetKit widgets, pulled forward from the 0.3 plan so app design work can start with this release. It decides whether a menu or a window is enough, keeps status items from redrawing on every appearance callback, gives widgets Edit Widget options through AppIntentConfiguration with AppEntity parameters, stops stale extension processes after a self-update, and tests widgets offscreen. Checkers `scan-swift.mjs` (MW-APPEAR-01, MW-CONFIG-01), the read-only `probe-extension.mjs` (MW-STALE-01) and `snapshot-widgets.mjs` (MW-SNAP-01, MW-SNAP-02; it renders with ImageRenderer inside 16 pt content margins with a pinned locale, time zone and calendar, builds in a scratch folder under `$TMPDIR`, byte-compares against the baseline folder passed with `--baseline`, which belongs with the project's tests, and skips off macOS); five Swift references type-checked with Swift 6; 5 error rules and 6 warnings; 35 red-first fixture cases; part evals 1 to 6 and routing cases.

It decides between a plain site, a home-screen web app and a Capacitor or Tauri shell, proves which origin a built shell loads, replaces HTTP Basic Auth with a login page and a session cookie, and tests cookies in WebKit over HTTPS. `check-shell.mjs` reads Capacitor configs (ts, then js, then json, top-level literal `server` only; computed values are skipped with a reason), native copies, built `.app` and unzipped `.ipa` bundles and Tauri configs with their platform overrides (WS-ORIGIN-01, WS-ORIGIN-02, WS-BUNDLE-01); `probe-webkit.mjs` drives Playwright's WebKit against a local self-signed HTTPS server whose certificate is made at run time (WS-AUTH-01, WS-COOKIE-01); a login reference that cannot be turned into an open redirect; 5 error rules and 4 warnings; part evals 31 to 36 and routing cases r55 to r57.
